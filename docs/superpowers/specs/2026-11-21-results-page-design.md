# Design: `paper/RESULTS.md`, the record of outcomes and decisions

*2026-11-21. Short design for the author's approval. Nothing is built yet.*

## Intent

One page that records, before the paper is drafted, what each prediction of
spec §5.4 came to and what the author decided on the open items of spec §11.
It is for the author while drafting and for a reader who wants to check the
paper against the data. Today that record is spread over the spec's dated
notes, `CLAUDE.md` and the tables.

The author asked for three things: replicability, transparency and ease of
access. The author also asked that the file be easy for a person to read
and to edit.

## Goals

- Every number on the page comes from a committed table under `paper/` and
  is regenerated by a command, never typed.
- Every verdict and every decision is written by the author, in the page,
  as plain text.
- A reader sees all verdicts on one screen, and under each one the numbers
  it rests on and the kind of support it has.
- The choice of which cells bear on which claim is visible and committed
  before any verdict is written.

## Not goals

- No new measure and no new test. Where no paired test exists, the page
  says so. A paired test is a separate branch (it is on the list of open
  gaps).
- No proposed verdicts. The page is delivered with every verdict and every
  decision blank.
- No pooled number across languages. Portuguese is shown apart.

## What would make it a failure

- The author has to edit YAML, or to read markup, to record a verdict.
- A regeneration changes or loses a word the author wrote.
- A number on the page differs from the table it cites.

## Approaches considered

**A. Pointers only, written by hand.** One row per claim with the file and
the cells to read, no number in the page. Nothing can drift and no code is
needed, but the reader opens the CSV beside the page to judge a claim.

**B. Evidence generated, verdicts by the author, one page.** Chosen by the
author on 2026-11-21, on the condition that the page is designed for a
human reader.

**C. Numbers typed by hand and checked by a command.** Dropped. Most useful
numbers are sums over categories, so the checker would recompute them,
which is the work of B without its benefit.

## How B is made readable (my calls, for the author to check)

1. **One file, written as an ordinary Markdown page.** There is no separate
   claims file. I had proposed one in YAML; this replaces it.
2. **Two kinds of text, with one rule.** Text between a pair of marker
   lines is generated and is overwritten at each run. All other text is the
   author's and is never touched. The markers are HTML comments, so they do
   not show in the preview. In the source each one says in words what it
   is:

       <!-- generated by `gricean results`: table=intent ladder=olmo language=en condition=full -->
       ...
       <!-- end of generated block: edits above this line are overwritten -->

3. **The marker says which cells it shows.** That line is the mapping from
   claim to cells. It sits next to the claim, and it is committed with
   empty blocks before the first run fills them.
4. **Tables are written for reading.** Rungs carry the names the paper uses
   (Stage 1, Base, SFT, DPO, Instruct; Stage 1 Alpaca, 500, 2,000, all
   pairs, format only). Line 1 cells are counts (`3/8`), with one total row
   over the categories (`7/48`), which is a sum of cells and the only
   number not in a table as such. Line 2 cells are the mean with its
   interval, two decimals (`0.43 [−0.03, 0.81]`). No pooled Line 2 mean is
   computed.
5. **Structure follows §5.4.** Four sections, one per bullet, each opening
   with the bullet quoted. The evidence for a bullet is shown once. Under
   it the bullet is split into its claims (twelve in all, listed below),
   and each claim has the same four lines for the author:

       - Line 1 verdict:
       - Line 2 verdict:
       - Decided on:
       - Note:

   A verdict is `held`, `failed` or `mixed`, or is left empty. The two
   lines are separate because the two measures are independent by design; a
   single verdict would be `mixed` whenever they disagree. A claim that
   only one line can speak to has one verdict line.
6. **A summary at the top**, generated from those lines: claim, Line 1
   verdict, Line 2 verdict, support. An empty verdict shows as "open".
7. **The command refuses, it does not guess.** `gricean results` rewrites
   the generated blocks and the summary. It writes nothing and names the
   line if a marker is unpaired, a marker asks for a table that does not
   exist, or a verdict is not one of the three words. `gricean results
   --check` changes nothing and reports whether the page is up to date.
   `make report` runs it last.
8. **Numbers are read from the CSV files under `paper/`**, not recomputed
   from the coded sheet, so the page cannot disagree with the tables the
   paper cites.

## The other sections of the page

- **Decisions (spec §11).** Three entries: the Instruct column; Base as a
  column or as a dose note; the pairs from Ouyang et al. (2022). Each has
  the question as the spec asks it, a pointer to the table that bears on
  it, and three lines for the author: `Decision:`, `Decided on:`,
  `Reason:`.
- **Analyses added after the results (spec §13.1).** Turn completion,
  marked post hoc, with its counts and the one exploratory comparison,
  generated from the `termination` files.
- **Agreement between the coders.** The three kappa values, generated from
  `kappa.txt`, with the note that it is one number for the whole sample.
- **What no table tests.** Written by hand from the open gaps: no paired
  test between rungs or of data scale on `correct`; turn completion cannot
  be read past SFT on the official ladder.
- **Questions from exploration.** A list for the author, each question to
  end as a remark made after seeing the data or as an analysis fixed before
  it is computed (`WORKFLOW.md` §5). It starts with the one question
  already on the list.
- **What the paper must disclose.** The five items of `WORKFLOW.md` §6.
- **Where the data comes from.** The commands, and pointers to the files
  that hold the stimuli checksum, the model pins and the seeds. No hash is
  copied into the page.

Portuguese: under each prediction a second, smaller table shows the
Portuguese cells (two items per category), labelled as a generalization
check. The verdict lines are for English; the `Note` line is the place for
a remark on Portuguese.

## Constraints

- Tests build their page and tables in `tmp_path`; none reads the real
  `paper/` folder.
- The package computes: the code is a module `gricean.results` and a CLI
  command, with no notebook.
- One branch and one pull request, merged with a merge commit. Order of
  commits: (1) module and tests; (2) the page with empty generated blocks
  and blank verdicts, and a dated note in the main spec saying that the
  split into claims and the choice of cells were made after the results;
  (3) the page as filled by the first run. The author then writes the
  verdicts, and `git diff` on the page is their dated record.

## Left to the builder

  name (at least `intent`, `logodds`, `kappa`, `termination`).
- Where the denominators of the counts come from (the frozen stimuli give

- Small edits that point to the page: `WORKFLOW.md` §1 and §3, `README.md`
  step 9, `CLAUDE.md` status.
